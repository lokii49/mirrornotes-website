An Apple Journal alternative that reads what you write

Apple Journal is free, simple, and genuinely private: it lives on your iPhone and nudges you to write using on-device suggestions from your photos, places, and activity. What it doesn't do is engage with your writing once it's down. MirrorNotes does: daily prompts drawn from your own patterns, mood analysis, weekly digests, and a journal you can ask questions, all on-device and free to start: writing is free forever, and the AI features are free until 15 November 2026.

TL;DR

  • Apple Journal = the simplest possible built-in journal. MirrorNotes = a journal that reads what you actually wrote.
  • Apple Journal suggests moments to write about (photos, places, workouts). MirrorNotes analyzes what you've already written and reflects it back, with prompts, a mood timeline, and "Ask your journal".
  • Both are free, and both can use Apple's on-device Foundation Models. MirrorNotes' AI layer — Foundation Models where available, its own bundled Gemma 3 model everywhere else — is the whole product: active mood analysis, digests, and Ask, not a writing-prompt add-on.
  • Want the built-in default with zero setup? Apple Journal is right there. Want a model that actually engages with your entries? Try MirrorNotes.

Different jobs: gentle nudge vs. active mirror

Apple Journal's strength is that it's already on your phone, costs nothing, and gets out of your way. Its suggestions are genuinely useful for building the habit: a photo from a hike, a workout you finished, a place you visited, each becomes a quiet invitation to write. It's private by default and it doesn't try to be more than a well-made journaling app. If that simplicity is what you want, Apple Journal is a strong, honest choice, and we won't pretend otherwise.

MirrorNotes starts where Apple Journal stops: after you've written something. A local model reads it, on your phone, and gives something back:

  • Daily Nudge: tomorrow morning's prompt is generated from what you've actually been writing about, not a photo from your camera roll.
  • Ask your journal: "when do I feel most alive?" "what am I avoiding?" Answered from months of your own entries.
  • Weekly Digest: every Sunday, the week's themes and moods summarized.
  • Mood Timeline: emotional trends you'd never notice reading day by day, with alerts when several entries in a row trend negative.
The privacy story is similar, but not identical: Apple Journal's on-device suggestions and MirrorNotes' on-device AI both mean your data never has to leave your phone for that part. The difference is what the AI actually does with your writing once it's there: Apple Journal doesn't read your entries to generate insight, and its broader Apple Intelligence writing tools can reach for cloud Private Cloud Compute on bigger requests. MirrorNotes' entire product is reading your writing to generate insight, and it never phones home to do it, whether it's using Apple's on-device Foundation Models or its own bundled Gemma 3 model.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use both?

Yes, and some people will. Apple Journal is a fine place to log moments with Photos and Health data attached; MirrorNotes is where you'd write to actually think something through and get a reflection back. MirrorNotes is free to write in, and its AI is free until 15 November 2026, so trying it costs nothing.

Can I import my Apple Journal entries?

Not yet, import is on the roadmap. New entries you write in MirrorNotes are yours: stored on-device in SwiftData and synced to your own iCloud.

Why iPhone only?

The on-device AI is the product. Modern iPhone chips run Apple's Foundation Models or Gemma 3 well; spreading thin across platforms would mean either cloud AI (breaking the privacy guarantee) or a worse experience. iPhone-first is a deliberate trade.
