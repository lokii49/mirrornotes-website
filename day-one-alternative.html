Different jobs: archive vs. mirror

Day One's strength is preservation. It's where a decade of photos, locations, and memories live, beautifully organized. If that's your journaling, Day One is genuinely hard to beat, and we won't pretend otherwise.

MirrorNotes is built for a different job: reflection. You write; the app reads what you wrote, on your phone, with a local model, and gives something back:

  • Daily Nudge: tomorrow morning's prompt is generated from what you've actually been writing about, not a quote-of-the-day.
  • Ask your journal: "when do I feel most alive?" "what am I avoiding?" Answered from months of your own entries.
  • Weekly Digest: every Sunday, the week's themes and moods summarized.
  • Mood Timeline: emotional trends you'd never notice reading day by day, with alerts when several entries in a row trend negative.
The privacy distinction matters here: an AI that reads your journal is exactly the kind of AI that should not run in the cloud. MirrorNotes' model runs on your chip. There is no server that ever sees your writing, not ours, not anyone's.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use both?

Plenty of people will. Day One as the long-term media archive; MirrorNotes as the daily writing and reflection habit. MirrorNotes is free and unlimited, so that costs nothing to try.

Can I import my Day One entries?

Not yet, import is on the roadmap. New entries you write in MirrorNotes are yours: stored on-device in SwiftData and synced to your own iCloud.

Why iPhone only?

The on-device AI is the product. Modern iPhone chips run Gemma 3 well; spreading thin across platforms would mean either cloud AI (breaking the privacy guarantee) or a worse experience. iPhone-first is a deliberate trade.
