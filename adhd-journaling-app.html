Guide

A journaling app that works the way an ADHD brain works

Thoughts arrive fast, in the wrong order, at the wrong time, and the insight you had two weeks ago is gone. MirrorNotes is built for exactly that: capture in seconds, tag the mood, and let an on-device AI remember the patterns so you don't have to.

Why ADHD brains like it

  • Zero-friction capture: the keyboard is up the moment the app opens. Type or talk.
  • Mood tags on every entry: see your actual emotional patterns, not your memory of them.
  • "Ask your journal" = external working memory: "what was I excited about last month?" answered from your own words.
  • One gentle daily prompt for structure. No guilt streaks, no notification spam.
  • 100% private: the AI runs on your iPhone. Your unmasked, unfiltered thoughts never reach a server.

The real problem isn't writing, it's retrieval

Most journaling advice fails ADHD brains at the same two points. First, friction: if capturing a thought takes four taps and a decision about which notebook it belongs in, the thought is gone. Second, retrieval: you did write it down somewhere, but rereading months of entries to find a pattern is exactly the kind of boring, sustained-attention task that doesn't happen.

MirrorNotes attacks both ends:

Capture in seconds

  • Open the app, keyboard is already up. No folders, no templates, no decisions. Brain-dump and close.
  • Voice input when typing is too much. Speech is transcribed on-device; audio is discarded.
  • Tag the mood in one tap: Anxious, Hyped, Drained, Calm, building a timeline as a side effect.

Retrieval without rereading

  • Ask your journal: "what keeps draining me?" "what was I hyperfocused on in May?" "when did I last feel like this?" The on-device AI answers from your actual entries.
  • Weekly Digest: Sunday summary of the week's themes and moods, the review you'd never do manually.
  • Mood Timeline: trends across weeks, visible at a glance, with an alert if several entries in a row trend negative.
Think of it as external working memory that talks back. You already externalize tasks into to-do apps. MirrorNotes does the same for thoughts and feelings, and unlike a paper journal, it can answer questions.

Structure without shame

One personalized prompt a day, the Daily Nudge, generated from what you've actually been writing about. It's a door, not a homework assignment. Skip a week and nothing scolds you; your history is intact and the app picks up where you are, not where a streak counter says you should be.

The app also deliberately waits until you've written a few entries before showing any AI insight. Generic advice on day one helps nobody; by day five, the nudges are about your life.

Private enough for the unfiltered version

A journal only works if you write the real thing: the rejection-sensitive spiral, the medication day-3 wall, the thing you can't say out loud. That's not text to send to a cloud AI.

MirrorNotes runs its AI entirely on your iPhone — Apple's own Foundation Models on Apple Intelligence-eligible iPhones, or the bundled Gemma 3 model via CoreML otherwise. There is no cloud AI backend, no API call, no account. Journal text never leaves the device; sync goes only to your own iCloud. The privacy guarantee is architectural: there's no network path for your words to take.

Honest note: MirrorNotes is a journaling tool, not a medical device, and isn't a substitute for ADHD treatment or therapy. It's the external-memory layer that sits alongside whatever else works for you.
