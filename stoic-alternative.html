A Stoic app alternative that keeps AI on your phone

Stoic is a well-built wellness app: mood tracking, CBT thought exercises, meditation and breathing sessions, and AI Mentors modeled on different coaching styles, wrapped in Stoic-philosophy prompts and quotes. To power its AI Mentors and insights, it shares your historical journal content with third-party AI services. MirrorNotes takes a narrower, on-device approach: daily prompts drawn from your own writing, mood analysis, weekly digests, and a journal you can ask questions, with the AI running entirely on your iPhone and free to start: writing is free forever, and the AI features are free until 15 November 2026.

TL;DR

  • Stoic = a full wellness toolkit (mood tracking, CBT exercises, meditation, 10 AI Mentor personas) built around cloud AI. MirrorNotes = a narrower journal that reflects your own writing back, with AI that never leaves your phone.
  • Stoic's AI features share your journal history with third-party AI providers to generate mentor responses and insights. MirrorNotes runs its AI locally on your iPhone — Apple's Foundation Models, or its bundled Gemma 3 model as a fallback — so there's no server or provider your entries are ever sent to.
  • Stoic's premium tier (most prompts, exercises, and AI Mentors) runs around $49.99/year. MirrorNotes is free, with unlimited entries and every AI feature included.
  • Want guided CBT exercises, meditations, and a choice of AI coaching personas? Stoic's toolkit is broader. Want your own words actually analyzed, on-device, at no cost? MirrorNotes is built for that.

Different jobs: a coaching program vs. an on-device mirror

Stoic's strength is breadth. It bundles Stoic philosophy (morning intention-setting, evening review, quotes from Marcus Aurelius), CBT-style thought exercises, meditation and breathing sessions, and AI Mentors that coach from different perspectives, all in one app with over 4 million users. If you want a structured self-improvement program with variety and guidance, Stoic offers more moving parts than MirrorNotes does, and we won't pretend otherwise.

MirrorNotes does one thing: it reads what you've actually written, on your phone, and reflects it back.

  • Daily Nudge: tomorrow morning's prompt is generated from what you've actually been writing about, not a template exercise.
  • Ask your journal: "when do I feel most alive?" "what am I avoiding?" Answered from months of your own entries, without any of that history leaving your device.
  • Weekly Digest: every Sunday, the week's themes and moods summarized.
  • Mood Timeline: emotional trends you'd never notice reading day by day.
The privacy story is the real difference: Stoic stores your entries on-device or in your personal iCloud by default, but using its AI Mentors and insights means sharing your historical journal content with third-party AI service providers over the network to generate those responses. MirrorNotes' AI — Apple's own Foundation Models, or its bundled Gemma 3 model as a fallback — runs entirely on your iPhone, so there is no server or provider your journal text is ever sent to, whether or not you use the AI features.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use both?

Yes. Stoic's meditation, breathing, and CBT exercises are a fine complement to a journal, and MirrorNotes is free to write in, and its AI is free until 15 November 2026, so trying it costs nothing.

Can I import my Stoic entries?

Not yet, import is on the roadmap. New entries you write in MirrorNotes are yours: stored on-device in SwiftData and synced to your own iCloud.

Why iPhone only?

The on-device AI is the product. Modern iPhone chips run Apple's Foundation Models or Gemma 3 well; spreading thin across platforms would mean either cloud AI (breaking the privacy guarantee) or a worse experience. iPhone-first is a deliberate trade.
