The core difference: where the AI lives

Rosebud's interactive coaching works by sending your words to large cloud models. That's how most AI journals work, and it's why they cost what they cost: every reflection you generate costs the company real server money, and that bill becomes your subscription price.

MirrorNotes makes the opposite bet: a small language model (Gemma 3 1B) runs directly on your iPhone's chip. The model reads your entries in your phone's memory, generates the insight, and nothing is transmitted. Two consequences follow:

  • Privacy is architectural, not a policy. There is no code path that sends journal text to a server. It can't leak, be subpoenaed from us, or train someone's model, we never have it.
  • There's no price floor at all. Our inference cost is zero, so the AI layer is free instead of $12.99/month.
Honest trade-off: a cloud model like the ones Rosebud uses is bigger and chattier than an on-device model. If you want long, back-and-forth coaching conversations, Rosebud does that better. If you want a private journal whose AI spots patterns, prompts you daily, and answers questions about your own writing, that's exactly what a local model is good at.

Frequently asked questions

Is MirrorNotes really free compared to Rosebud?

Yes. MirrorNotes is free versus Rosebud's $12.99/month, roughly $155/year. The gap is structural: we don't run AI servers, so there's no inference bill to pass on to you.

Does the on-device AI actually work as well?

For journaling, yes. Spotting patterns in your own writing doesn't need a frontier model, it needs your entries and a competent small model. Where you'd notice the difference is open-ended coaching chat, which MirrorNotes deliberately doesn't do.

Can I import my Rosebud entries?

Not yet, import tools are on the roadmap. Your MirrorNotes entries are stored in SwiftData on your device and synced to your own iCloud, so they remain yours either way.
