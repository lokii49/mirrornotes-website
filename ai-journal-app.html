What makes an app an "AI journal app"?

The label covers a wide range of apps, but the common thread is that the app does more than store text: it reads what you wrote and generates something back, a prompt, a mood score, a summary, an answer to a question. That reading step is where the apps differ most.

Cloud-based AI journal apps send your entry to a server running a large model (frequently a third-party API like OpenAI's), get a response, and display it. That's simple to build and produces strong results, but it means your private writing is transmitted and processed off-device, often by a company that isn't the one you downloaded the app from.

MirrorNotes takes the on-device route. Gemma 3 runs locally via CoreML on your iPhone's Neural Engine. It's a smaller model than the cloud giants, but it's tuned for exactly this job: reading your own entries and reflecting them back, without a network call.

Frequently asked questions

Is on-device AI as good as cloud AI?

For general-purpose tasks, large cloud models are more capable. For a narrow job, reading your own entries and reflecting patterns back, a well-tuned small model like Gemma 3 does the job without the round trip, the server cost, or the privacy trade-off.

Do I need an account to use the AI features?

No. There's no sign-in and no account. Open the app and write; the AI features unlock automatically once there's enough of your own writing to draw from.

Can I turn the AI off?

You can simply ignore the prompts and Ask feature and use MirrorNotes as a plain journal. Nothing about the AI is required to write and keep entries.

Is there a free trial or paywall?

No. Every AI feature, unlimited entries, Daily Nudge, Ask, Weekly Digest, Mood Timeline, is free with no subscription.
